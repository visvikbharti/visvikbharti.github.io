Project Overview

This page describes the computational analysis I did for a PhD study in Dr. Debojyoti Chakraborty's lab at CSIR-IGIB. The study asked which DNA-repair genes matter for accurately repairing a Cas9-made double-strand break, and whether the answer differs between staggered (overhanging) and blunt DNA ends. Knowing this could help make genome editing more precise.

A lab colleague led the study as her PhD project (Chapter 3 of her thesis): she carried out the experiments and generated the single-cell RNA-seq data. Her HEK293T cells expressed a catalytically dead Cas9 repressor (dCas9-KRAB) and carried a pooled CRISPRi library of guides against DNA-repair genes. She transfected them with a fluorescent ZsGreen reporter plasmid that had been cut beforehand to leave staggered ends (with en1FnCas9, an engineered FnCas9 variant) or blunt ends (with SpCas9). She then sorted the cells that had accurately repaired the reporter from those that had not, and profiled them by BD Rhapsody single-cell RNA-seq. An earlier single-cell analysis of the data is also acknowledged in the thesis.

From October 2024 to January 2025 I re-analysed the data, starting from the BD Rhapsody count matrices. I did the quality control, integration and differential expression in Seurat, compared gene sets between the staggered- and blunt-end conditions, and ran Gene Ontology enrichment. I also made the heat map and volcano plots in her thesis (Fig. 3.8, credited to me).

The analysis compares sorted populations, not individual cells. Each condition was a single sample, cells were not linked to their CRISPRi guide, and the candidate genes it points to still need experimental validation.

Separately, in May–June 2024, I did an analysis for another chapter of the same thesis. I screened ClinVar's pathogenic 1-, 2- and 3-bp deletions (the May-2024 release, on GRCh38) for an NGG PAM placed so that a Cas9 cut followed by templated fill-in could, in principle, re-add the lost bases, and made the plots for it, re-plotted for the thesis in January 2025 (Fig. 5.2 of her thesis, credited to me). It is a census of where a suitable PAM exists on the reference plus strand; it does not model the repair product or check the protein.

Computational Pipeline

The single-cell processing was a scripted Seurat 4 pipeline on the institute's HPC cluster. I did the gene-set comparisons and figures in Python and the enrichment in DAVID.

1

Quality Control and Integration

Per-sample cell filters (500–6,500 genes, 1,000–45,000 UMIs and under 12% mitochondrial reads per cell), log-normalisation, and Seurat's standard CCA-anchor integration of the five samples, with conservative settings.

2

Differential Expression

Seurat FindMarkers (Wilcoxon rank-sum test) comparing each sorted population with the control sample.

3

Gene-Set Comparison

Among the CRISPRi library genes, I took those expressed at lower levels than in the control in cells that had not repaired the reporter, checked their status in the cells that had repaired it, and compared the resulting lists between staggered and blunt ends.

4

Enrichment and Figures

Gene Ontology over-representation analysis (DAVID) of the resulting lists, and the heat map, volcano plots and Venn diagrams, made in Python.

Limits: each condition was a single sample, so cells served as replicates; differential expression was run on the integrated (batch-corrected) data; and because individual cells were not linked to their CRISPRi guide, every comparison is between sorted populations. The gene lists are candidates, not tested dependencies. An exploratory three-step filter I tried in December 2024 was superseded and is not part of these results.

Technical Implementation

Data

  • Samples: Five sample-tagged populations of HEK293T CRISPRi cells (dCas9-KRAB with a pooled DNA-repair guide library): a control, the cells that did (FZ2+) or did not (FZ2−) accurately repair a reporter cut with en1FnCas9 to leave staggered ends, and the same two populations for a reporter cut with SpCas9 to leave blunt ends (SZ2+, SZ2−)
  • Platform: BD Rhapsody whole-transcriptome single-cell RNA-seq; I started from the BD pipeline's count matrices

Computational Tools

  • R 4.1.0 with Seurat 4.0.4 (institute HPC): QC, normalisation, integration, clustering, UMAP and differential expression
  • DAVID (web Functional Annotation Chart): Gene Ontology enrichment
  • Python (pandas, NumPy, matplotlib, seaborn, matplotlib-venn, adjustText): Gene-set comparisons and figures

Methods

  • Normalisation: LogNormalize
  • Integration: Seurat CCA anchors (FindIntegrationAnchors and IntegrateData)
  • Differential expression: Wilcoxon rank-sum test, each sorted population against the control
  • Enrichment: Over-representation against DAVID's whole-genome background

Why It Matters

Cas9 enzymes leave breaks with different end structures, and how cells repair staggered versus blunt breaks had not been systematically investigated. Knowing which repair factors each end type relies on could help steer repair towards precise, template-directed outcomes in therapeutic genome editing. The candidate genes from this analysis still need experimental validation.

Future Research Directions

The question this work led to: SpCas9 leaves predominantly blunt DNA ends, while en1FnCas9, an engineered FnCas9 variant, leaves a staggered 5′ overhang of two to three bases, and the lab has reported higher rates of homology-directed repair with FnCas9 and en1FnCas9. The question I want to work on sits one level above pathway choice: after a Cas9 break in the genome, whether a cell repairs at all, or dies, and whether that decision is made cell by cell or coordinated across neighbours. The single-cell data I analysed cannot answer it: there the break was on a reporter plasmid cut before transfection, quality control removed cells with high mitochondrial read fractions (the usual sign of dying cells), and no cell-death analysis was run.
