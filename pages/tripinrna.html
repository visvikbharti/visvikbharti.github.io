TRIPinRNA

Python RNA Biology Bioinformatics Transcriptomics X Chromosome Inactivation
View Publication Code

Project Overview

TRIPinRNA is a Python-based in silico platform that searches whole transcriptomes for sequences that could fold into intramolecular RNA triple helices. In the human transcriptome only two such structures were well known, the stability elements at the 3′ ends of the lncRNAs MALAT1 and NEAT1, and the study asked whether triple helices are really that rare or had been missed by the search methods used so far.

As co-first author, I co-developed the search code and performed the bioinformatics analysis, and co-wrote the paper. Collaborators carried out the biophysical validation, and the work was co-supervised by Dr. Souvik Maiti.

Transcriptome-Wide Search

Scans every transcript, noncoding and protein-coding, for the three strands of a triple helix

Two Search Modes

With a gap nucleotide in the Hoogsteen strand, as in MALAT1 and NEAT1, or with a continuous strand

Complete Output

Every predicted triplex, with its strand sequences and start and end positions, written to a CSV file

Research Impact

The Biochemistry paper ran TRIPinRNA on the GENCODE human transcriptome (release 44), long noncoding and protein-coding, and on part of the NONCODE lncRNA database, and tested selected predictions by biophysics.

X Chromosome Inactivation

Predicted triplex-forming sequences in six lncRNAs involved in X-chromosome inactivation: XIST, TSIX, JPX, FTX, FIRRE and XACT. One predicted triplex each from JPX, FTX and XACT, made as a synthetic RNA, formed a triple helix in vitro.

Coding Transcripts and UTRs

In protein-coding transcripts more than half of the predicted hits lay in 3′ UTRs. Transcripts with hits had higher GC content in their 5′ UTRs and lower in their 3′ UTRs than average, a bias absent from their coding sequences.

Beyond Existing Tools

Several earlier triplex search programs allow at most 10 nucleotides between strands, too few for MALAT1-like triple helices, and one that allows longer gaps (NeSSie) is too lenient to give a workable number of hits; TRIPinRNA allows 3 to 100. An earlier Infernal-based search found only MALAT1 and NEAT1 in the human genome, and AlphaFold3 did not predict triple helices in TRIPinRNA's hits.

Experimental Validation

Collaborators validated selected predictions with circular dichroism, UV melting and thermal difference spectra, as reported in the publication.

Technical Approach

TRIPinRNA is a sequence-pattern (string-match) search modelled on the MALAT1 and NEAT1 triple helices:

1

Core Triplex Search

A sliding window looks for a Hoogsteen strand of 7 to 15 nucleotides, a downstream Crick strand that matches it, and a further downstream Watson strand complementary to both, with 3 to 100 nucleotides between the Hoogsteen and Crick strands and between the Crick and Watson strands.

2

Gap and No-Gap Modes

In “with-gap” mode the Hoogsteen strand carries one gap nucleotide, placed anywhere except the three nucleotides at either end, as in MALAT1 and NEAT1; “without-gap” mode looks for a continuous strand.

3

Stems and Loops

Once the core triplex is found, the sequences between the strands are checked for an upper and a lower stem of at least 3 base pairs with a loop of at least 3 nucleotides; without a stem, the segment is reported as a loop. No base-composition rule is imposed, so canonical and noncanonical triplexes are both found, and pyrimidine-only and uridine-only hits are filtered afterwards.

4

Output and Scale

The input is a zipped FASTA file and the output a CSV of every predicted triplex. The transcriptome runs were executed on an HPC cluster under the SLURM scheduler, and the code for both modes is on GitHub.

Key Findings

Thousands of Candidates

In a human transcriptome where essentially only the MALAT1 and NEAT1 triple helices were well known, the without-gap search predicted 10,769 candidate triplex sites in lncRNA transcripts and tens of thousands in protein-coding transcripts.

Noncanonical Triplexes Form In Vitro

Heteropurine-pyrimidine triple helices, usually expected to be destabilizing, gave triple-helix signatures in vitro, though they were less thermally stable than canonical ones.

Link to GC Content

Transcripts with predicted triplexes differed in GC content from the transcriptome average: lncRNA transcripts with hits had slightly lower GC (mean 44.5–44.8% against 46.6%), and coding transcripts with hits had higher GC in their 5′ UTRs and lower GC in their 3′ UTRs.

A Canonical Triplex in JPX

A uridine-only triple helix predicted in JPX, the lncRNA that activates XIST, melted in two transitions (about 58 and 75 °C) and gave a CD signature practically identical to that of the MALAT1 triple helix.

Publication

Development of an in silico platform (TRIPinRNA) for the identification of novel RNA intramolecular triple helices and their validation using biophysical techniques

Isha Rakheja*, Vishal Bharti*, S Sahana, Prosad Kumar Das, Gyan Ranjan, Ajit Kumar, Niyati Jain, and Souvik Maiti
*Equal contribution.

Biochemistry (ACS) 64(1):250-265 (2025). DOI: 10.1021/acs.biochem.4c00334

Summary: TRIPinRNA is a Python tool that scans human long noncoding and protein-coding transcripts for sequence patterns that could fold into intramolecular RNA triple helices. With it, the study predicts both canonical (homopyrimidine) and noncanonical (heteropurine-pyrimidine) triplexes, shows with biophysical tests on selected candidates that both kinds form in vitro, and reports a strong correlation between triplex occurrence and local GC content. The tool can be applied to any organism with an annotated transcriptome.

Future Directions

The paper points to these next steps:

Learning From the Predictions

Using TRIPinRNA's output to train machine-learning models that find triple helices with slightly different features.

Other Organisms

Extending the search to the transcriptomes of other organisms; the code works on any annotated transcriptome.

What Governs a Triplex

Relating loop length, strand length and the presence of a stem to when triple helices form and how stable they are, and asking whether triplexes predicted in coding sequences relate to nonsense-mediated decay.

Structures of Noncanonical Triplexes

Crystal structures of the noncanonical triple helices, which the paper notes are beyond its scope.
