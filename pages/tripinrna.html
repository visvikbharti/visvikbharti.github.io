TRIPinRNA

View Publication GitHub

Project Overview

TRIPinRNA is a Python-based in silico platform that searches whole transcriptomes for sequences that could fold into intramolecular RNA triple helices. In the human transcriptome only two such structures were well known, the stability elements at the 3′ ends of the lncRNAs MALAT1 and NEAT1, and the study asked whether triple helices are really that rare or had simply not been looked for.

As co-first author, I co-developed the search code and performed the bioinformatics analysis, and co-wrote the paper. Collaborators carried out the biophysical validation, and the work was co-supervised by Dr. Souvik Maiti.

Transcriptome-Wide Search

Scans every transcript, noncoding and protein-coding, for the three strands of a triple helix

Two Search Modes

With a gap nucleotide in the Hoogsteen strand, as in MALAT1 and NEAT1, or with a continuous strand

Complete Output

Every predicted triplex, with its strand sequences and start and end positions, written to a CSV file

Research Impact

Our publication in Biochemistry demonstrates the tool's capabilities and its applications in understanding complex regulatory mechanisms.

X Chromosome Inactivation

Identified potential triple helix structures in XIST RNA that may play roles in silencing the X chromosome. These predictions provide new avenues for experimental validation and deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms involved in dosage compensation.

Gene Regulation Mechanisms

Discovered triple helix structures in the 3' UTRs of various genes, suggesting roles in post-transcriptional regulation. These findings contribute to our understanding of how RNA structure influences gene expression and cellular function.

Methodological Advances

Developed novel computational approaches for predicting RNA triple helices, addressing limitations in existing methods. The algorithms incorporate sequence complementarity, structural constraints, and thermodynamic considerations.

Experimental Validation

Predicted triple helices were validated by collaborators using biophysical techniques, as reported in the publication.

Technical Approach

TRIPinRNA is a sequence-pattern (string-match) search modelled on the MALAT1 and NEAT1 triple helices:

1

Core Triplex Search

A sliding window looks for a Hoogsteen strand of 7 to 15 nucleotides, a downstream Crick strand that matches it, and a further downstream Watson strand complementary to both, with 3 to 100 nucleotides between the Hoogsteen and Crick strands and between the Crick and Watson strands.

2

Gap and No-Gap Modes

In “with-gap” mode the Hoogsteen strand carries one gap nucleotide, placed anywhere except the three nucleotides at either end, as in MALAT1 and NEAT1; “without-gap” mode looks for a continuous strand.

3

Stems and Loops

Once the core triplex is found, the sequences between the strands are checked for an upper and a lower stem of at least 3 base pairs with a loop of at least 3 nucleotides; without a stem, the segment is reported as a loop. No base-composition rule is imposed, so canonical and noncanonical triplexes are both found, and pyrimidine-only and uridine-only hits are filtered afterwards.

4

Output and Scale

The input is a zipped FASTA file and the output a CSV of every predicted triplex. The transcriptome runs were executed on an HPC cluster under the SLURM scheduler, and the code for both modes is on GitHub.

Key Findings

Triple Helix Prevalence

Discovered that triple helices are more prevalent in the human transcriptome than previously thought, with particular enrichment in long non-coding RNAs involved in gene regulation.

Functional Associations

Identified associations between triple helix structures and specific functional roles, including chromatin interactions, RNA-protein binding, and RNA stability regulation.

Evolutionary Conservation

Observed significant evolutionary conservation of triple helix forming sequences across mammalian species, suggesting important functional roles that have been preserved.

Novel RNA Motifs

Discovered novel sequence motifs associated with triple helix formation that were not previously characterized in the literature, expanding our understanding of RNA structures.

Publication

Development of an in silico platform (TRIPinRNA) for the identification of novel RNA intramolecular triple helices and their validation using biophysical techniques

Isha Rakheja*, Vishal Bharti*, S Sahana, Prosad Kumar Das, Gyan Ranjan, Ajit Kumar, Niyati Jain, and Souvik Maiti
*Equal contribution.

Biochemistry (ACS) 64(1):250-265 (2024). DOI: 10.1021/acs.biochem.4c00334

Summary: RNA molecules can form a variety of secondary and tertiary structures that play important roles in cellular processes. One such structure is the triple helix, where a third strand interacts with a duplex through Hoogsteen or reverse Hoogsteen base pairing. In this study, we present TRIPinRNA, a computational platform for predicting intramolecular RNA triple helices. The platform incorporates algorithms for sequence complementarity analysis, structural constraint evaluation, and thermodynamic stability calculation. We validate our predictions using biophysical techniques including circular dichroism spectroscopy and thermal melting experiments. Our findings reveal that triple helices are more prevalent in the human transcriptome than previously recognized, with particular enrichment in long non-coding RNAs involved in gene regulation. This platform provides researchers with a valuable resource for investigating the functional roles of RNA triple helices in various biological contexts.

Future Directions

The development of TRIPinRNA opens up several exciting avenues for future research:

Enhanced Prediction Algorithms

Continuing to refine the prediction algorithms by incorporating deep learning approaches and additional experimental data. These improvements will enhance the accuracy and reliability of triple helix predictions.

Expanded Database

Building a comprehensive database of RNA triple helices across diverse organisms, providing a valuable resource for comparative studies and evolutionary analyses. This database will facilitate the identification of conserved structural patterns.

Integration with Other Tools

Developing integrations with other RNA structure prediction tools to create a more comprehensive platform for RNA structural analysis. This integration will provide researchers with a more complete picture of RNA folding and structure.

Experimental Validation Pipeline

Creating a systematic pipeline for experimental validation of predicted triple helices, including protocols for in vitro and cellular assays. This pipeline will help bridge the gap between computational predictions and biological reality.
